These terms apply when you offer creative work on SLAPorSLOP People (a **creative**) or hire someone there (a **client**), on top of the Terms of Service and the Content Policy. You must be 18 or older.

## 1. SLAPorSLOP is in the middle, not a party
- Each job is a contract between the creative and the client: the **Service Agreement** shown on the job page. SLAPorSLOP isn't a party to it.
- SLAPorSLOP provides the directory, the board of open requests, the job page, the recorded conversation, and the payment page.
- Creatives are independent. They set their own prices, scope, dates and methods, and choose which work they take. SLAPorSLOP doesn't employ them, assign work, or rank them by how much work they accept.
- On the board of open requests, SLAPorSLOP doesn't match, rank, recommend or assign anyone, doesn't tell creatives about particular requests, and sets no prices. Nobody has to answer a request or a name put forward; a request closes as section 9 says.

## 2. Exact wording
The Service Agreement binds both sides to its words: the creative delivers what's written, nothing more and nothing less. Any change after acceptance is a **Change to Agreement**: a new document with its own price and date that both sides accept.

## 3. Payments and fees
- The client pays the creative directly through Stripe, on the creative's own Stripe account. SLAPorSLOP never holds the money.
- SLAPorSLOP's fee is **5%** of each payment, taken from what the creative receives. Nothing is added to the client's price.
- **SLAPorSLOP's fee promise:** the fee will never be more than 5%. Any other change to fees that costs creatives more takes effect at least 12 months after SLAPorSLOP announces it, instead of the usual 15 days.
- Refunds are issued by the creative.
- Creatives are responsible for their own taxes. SLAPorSLOP may have to report creatives' income to tax authorities (EU DAC7, US 1099-K).

## 4. Delivery
Delivery before the last payment is a preview. The client may use the work only once it's paid in full. After delivery, the client has 7 days to accept it or ask for a change; after that, it counts as accepted, but not while a problem about it is open. Completing a job never shortens a consumer's legal right to cancel.

## 5. Conversations
Job conversations are recorded for contract and legal purposes. The creative opens them; the client can ask at any time. SLAPorSLOP keeps the text while the conversation is open and for 30 days after it closes, then keeps only fingerprints that prove a downloaded copy is unchanged. Download your copy.

## 6. Problems and mediation
Either side can open a problem on the job page. Both state their case and propose outcomes; anything both accept is carried out. A SLAPorSLOP mediator may suggest an outcome, which binds no one. **SLAPorSLOP doesn't decide who's right.** If you don't agree, the client can use their card issuer's dispute process, and either side can go to court. If you're a consumer, you keep your legal rights.

## 7. Reviews
Clients can review a finished job. Reviews are never removed for being negative. A creative can dispute a review that breaks the rules (insults, private information, not about the job, used as leverage, or false facts); a mediator decides, and gives the reason.

## 8. Rule breaches
SLAPorSLOP acts on breaches of its own rules (scams, repeated non-delivery, fraud, illegal content) and may hide a profile or an open request, or suspend an account, with a statement of reasons and the right to appeal.

## 9. Open requests
- A client can post an open request on People: what one of their projects in the store needs, with one skill, a title, a description and, if they like, the date they hope to have it. A request is public, with the client's display name and the project. It has no budget: the creative sets the price in their offer.
- A creative can put their name forward for someone else's request that is shown to them on the board, when they have accepted these terms, their People page is live, they are taking paid work with payouts set up, and their page lists the request's skill. Those are the only conditions, apart from a daily limit that stops spam and a block between the two people: someone a client has blocked, or who has blocked the client, isn't shown that client's requests while signed in and can't put their name forward for them.
- The client then sees that creative's card from the People directory, with a link to their page, when they first put their name forward, and the package they named, if they named one and it's still on their page. The client sees the name only while the creative's People page is live and there's no block between them. Nothing else about the creative is sent, and the creative can take their name back.
- On the board, requests are listed newest first, and the client sees names in the order they were first put forward, 100 at a time.
- Putting a name forward is not an offer, and a request is not a job: a job starts only with a hire request from the client, an offer from the creative and an accepted Service Agreement.
- A client can't post a new request while three of theirs are open, and there's a daily limit on new requests. A request closes after 30 days, or sooner if the client closes it. If SLAPorSLOP puts a request back after an appeal while it is still open, it stays open for at least 7 days from then, or until its old closing time if that is later, unless the client closes it. A request isn't shown while its project isn't in the store or the client's account is suspended. A client who owes money for finished work can't post a request until it's paid.
- A request that breaks the Content Policy can be hidden (see section 8).

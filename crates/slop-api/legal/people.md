These terms apply when you offer creative work on Slop People (a **creative**) or hire someone there (a **client**), on top of the Terms of Service and the Content Policy. You must be 18 or older.

## 1. Slop is in the middle, not a party
- Each job is a contract between the creative and the client: the **Service Agreement** shown on the job page. Slop isn't a party to it.
- Slop provides the directory, the job page, the recorded conversation, and the payment page.
- Creatives are independent. They set their own prices, scope, dates and methods, and choose which work they take. Slop doesn't employ them, assign work, or rank them by how much work they accept.

## 2. Exact wording
The Service Agreement binds both sides to its words: the creative delivers what's written, nothing more and nothing less. Any change after acceptance is a **Change to Agreement**: a new document with its own price and date that both sides accept.

## 3. Payments and fees
- The client pays the creative directly through Stripe, on the creative's own Stripe account. Slop never holds the money.
- Slop's fee is **5%** of each payment, taken from what the creative receives. Nothing is added to the client's price.
- **Slop's fee promise:** the fee will never be more than 5%. Any other change to fees that costs creatives more takes effect at least 12 months after Slop announces it, instead of the usual 15 days.
- Refunds are issued by the creative.
- Creatives are responsible for their own taxes. Slop may have to report creatives' income to tax authorities (EU DAC7, US 1099-K).

## 4. Delivery
Delivery before the last payment is a preview. The client may use the work only once it's paid in full. After delivery, the client has 7 days to accept it or ask for a change; after that, it counts as accepted, but not while a problem about it is open. Completing a job never shortens a consumer's legal right to cancel.

## 5. Conversations
Job conversations are recorded for contract and legal purposes. The creative opens them; the client can ask at any time. Slop keeps the text while the conversation is open and for 30 days after it closes, then keeps only fingerprints that prove a downloaded copy is unchanged. Download your copy.

## 6. Problems and mediation
Either side can open a problem on the job page. Both state their case and propose outcomes; anything both accept is carried out. A Slop mediator may suggest an outcome, which binds no one. **Slop doesn't decide who's right.** If you don't agree, the client can use their card issuer's dispute process, and either side can go to court. If you're a consumer, you keep your legal rights.

## 7. Reviews
Clients can review a finished job. Reviews are never removed for being negative. A creative can dispute a review that breaks the rules (insults, private information, not about the job, used as leverage, or false facts); a mediator decides, and gives the reason.

## 8. Rule breaches
Slop acts on breaches of its own rules (scams, repeated non-delivery, fraud, illegal content) and may hide a profile or suspend an account, with a statement of reasons and the right to appeal.
